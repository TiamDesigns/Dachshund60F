## Before ordering boards

- **Fab preview of the USB connector edges (review).** The daughterboard and dongle each keep two stock USB-C connector silkscreen/edge warnings. Check them in the fab's preview before ordering (the connector overhangs the edge on purpose).

## Firmware and app, testable before boards arrive (in phase order)

- **Encoder on real hardware (bring-up).** Check that one click is one step with `CONFIG_KB_ENCODER_STEPS_PER_DETENT=4` and that clockwise is clockwise; if not, change the setting or swap A and B in the devicetree.
- **Matrix test mode and a Doghouse key tester.** Protocol v1 has the `matrix_stream` capability bit but no command for it. Add one (a new command isn't a breaking change), stream raw matrix state while Doghouse's key tester is open, and light up pressed keys. The bring-up plan's smoke test and every-key check want it.
- **Firmware update on real hardware.** Run Doghouse's About → Update firmware… against a board (or an nRF52840 dongle running the Adafruit bootloader): the drive picker must accept the DACHSHUND drive's root, the write must complete before the bootloader resets, and the board must come back over WebHID by itself. Check Windows and macOS.
- **Bluetooth on real hardware** (a Pup covers most of this before the boards arrive). Pair with Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS and Android; check that NKRO and the media keys work over Bluetooth on each (fall back to 6KRO over Bluetooth if a host misbehaves), that unplugging the cable moves typing to Bluetooth without a stuck key, that BT1-BT3 and BT Clr behave, and that Doghouse finds the board over Bluetooth in Chrome (WebHID) with an ATT MTU of at least 67. Measure the key-to-host latency.
- **Undo for advanced keys, macros, settings and colours.** Doghouse's undo and redo cover keys and encoders only; the Advanced, Macros and Settings tabs apply straight to the board (Revert still undoes everything unsaved).
- **Power management on real hardware (phase 13).** With the PPK2 at 3.7 V on J2 (bring-up plan, Stage 4), measure the current while typing, idle with the lights on, lights out, advertising paused and in deep sleep. Check that a key wakes the board from deep sleep and how long it takes to type again over Bluetooth, and that the LED walk is skipped after a wake (if the UF2 bootloader clears the reset reason, keep a wake marker in GPREGRET2 instead). Check the gauge against a real cell (VDDH/5 reading, the curve in `kb/battery.c`) and CHG_STAT while charging. Tune `CONFIG_KB_IDLE_MS`, `CONFIG_KB_LIGHTS_OFF_S` and `CONFIG_KB_ADVERTISING_OFF_S` from the results.
- **2.4 GHz dongle on real hardware (phase 14).** Flash an nRF52840 Dongle (`dongle/README.md`) and pair it with a board (or a Pup: a second nRF52840 Dongle running the keyboard firmware, `boards/Pup/README.md`). Check: pairing from the dongle button plus `RF_PAIR` and from Doghouse; typing with `OUT_2G4`; lock LEDs following the computer; Doghouse reaching the keyboard through the dongle, and showing nothing (then the board again) while the keyboard sleeps; unplugging the dongle mid-keypress leaves nothing stuck. Measure range through a desk, dropouts next to busy 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, key-to-USB latency (logic analyser on a column and the USB D+ line, or the bring-up plan's end-to-end method), the keyboard's current while polling at 10 ms and 100 ms, and typing over the dongle while Bluetooth stays connected (MPSL timeslots). If 2475 MHz is noisy, add channel hopping.
- **Pup on real hardware.** Flash a PCA10059 with the Pup build (`boards/Pup/README.md`) and check: SW1 types `a`; LD2 walks red, green, blue at power-up and then follows Caps Lock over USB, Bluetooth and the dongle; Doghouse finds it as "Pup" and remaps the key; holding SW1 for 10 s enters Nordic's bootloader (GPREGRET 0xB1; if it only reboots, that bootloader doesn't take GPREGRET and the reset button is the way in); settings survive an nrfutil update.
- **Encrypt the 2.4 GHz link.** Keys over the dongle are sent in the clear. Exchange a key at pairing (ECDH on both nRF52840s, or a key from the dongle in the PAIR_OFFER, accepting the pairing-window risk), then encrypt each packet with AES-CCM (the nRF52840's CCM peripheral, or the ECB block with a packet counter), with replay protection. Store the key with the dongle address on the keyboard and in the dongle's settings. Bump nothing in the config protocol; the link format in `kb/link.h` changes, so keyboard and dongle must update together.
- **Dongle firmware updates.** The dongle is flashed with nrfutil today. For the Dachshund dongle PCB, settle its bootloader (see the open decision in `PROGRESS.md`), then let Doghouse update it like the keyboard (a reboot-to-bootloader command over the dongle's own config interface, which today relays everything to the keyboard).
- **Set up the Doghouse update key (Tiam).** Releases up to 0.1.3 were built without it, so they can only point to a download, not install updates. Make the signing key and add the secret and variable as in `app/src-tauri/README.md`, then bump the version, run the "Doghouse release" workflow and check that the release has `latest.json` and `.sig` files. Install that one by hand, then release once more and check that the installed copy offers the update in its sidebar and installs it on each OS.
- **Doghouse desktop app on real hardware.** On Windows, macOS and Linux: a board (or a Pup) shows up over USB with no Connect step; over Bluetooth (hidapi's BLE support differs by OS); through the dongle, including the wait while its keyboard is away; a firmware update finds the UF2 drive and writes it. On Linux check the udev rule from the .deb.
- **Code signing for the desktop app.** The installers are unsigned, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper warn. Windows: an Authenticode certificate (or Azure Trusted Signing) and `bundle.windows.signCommand` or the certificate settings in `app/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`. macOS: an Apple Developer ID, then the `APPLE_CERTIFICATE`, `APPLE_CERTIFICATE_PASSWORD`, `APPLE_SIGNING_IDENTITY`, `APPLE_ID`, `APPLE_PASSWORD` and `APPLE_TEAM_ID` secrets passed to tauri-action in `doghouse-release.yml` for signing and notarisation. Both cost money each year, so it's Tiam's call.
- **Input delay on real hardware.** Read Doghouse's About → Input timing after a minute of typing (matrix read time, key-to-report time, how many scans started on a key press), and compare with the end-to-end method in Stage 4 of the bring-up plan. If the matrix read takes long, try a lower `CONFIG_KB_MATRIX_SETTLE_US`.

- **USB edge cases on real hardware (review).** Pull the cable and stall the host mid-typing (a busy VM, a KVM switch) and check no key stays held and taps in a burst aren't lost. Check what Zephyr's HID class does with SET_IDLE (a non-zero idle rate needs a periodic resend, which the firmware doesn't do), unplug and replug during heavy typing (interface teardown against a report in flight), and typing in a BIOS/UEFI setup screen (boot protocol).
- **Doghouse session tests against the real session module (review).** `serial.test.ts` covers the queue, but `BoardSession` itself (`app-state.svelte.ts`, Svelte runes) has no tests. Compile it with Svelte's `compileModule` in a test, run it against a mock board with delays and injected failures, and cover: two overlapping edits then undo, a failed undo or redo, an edit straight after revert or reset, and unplugging during a reload.
- **KiCad checks in CI (review).** Run ERC, and DRC with schematic parity, on all four boards with a pinned KiCad (the `kicad/kicad:10.0` image). Fail on new active errors and unconnected items only; keep the seven spacebar exclusions and report active and excluded entries separately. Then the STEP export and the plate could be checked against the PCB in the same job.

## Tiam's own items

- **Make and fit the case.** Get the case from `hardware/case/` made: aluminium top and bottom, the polycarbonate side panel and the switch cap (a printed prototype first is cheaper; it's 336.5 mm long, so use a print service or split it). Check how much the socks squeeze (`SOCK_SQUEEZE`), the tongue and groove's fit at the seam (and the tapped M3 holes: open them to 4.2 mm for heat-set inserts if the prototype is printed), the USB-C plug lead-in against real cables, the switch cap's fit on SW1's actuator and its travel, the reset pin hole, the screw-in stabilizers over the 3.5 mm foam, the battery tray against the cell you buy, the encoder body under the hump, the roller's clearance in its slot, whether the wheel's click is comfortable to push from the end, and the radio's range with the aluminium top over the antenna. Make a 19 × 23.5 mm roller. Buy the rubber strip (10 × 3 mm, self-adhesive solid rubber, about 0.6 m), 1.5 mm black closed-cell foam for the LED seal, 1 mm opal acrylic (or frosted film) and a 4 mm punch for the LED diffusers, and a JST-SH 4-pin cable of about 200 mm for the centred USB-C board. Check the roller's bore against the shaft.
- **Dongle enclosure.** Still to design: plastic, with nothing metal over the antenna strip.
- **Display.** The case is built for a bare 2.13" e-paper panel (GoodDisplay GDEY0213B74) on end, driven by an Adafruit eInk Breakout Friend (#4224). Buy both so they arrive with the boards, measure the panel (where the picture sits along it) and the Friend (size, height of its parts), and correct `DISPLAYS` in `hardware/case/gen_case.py` before printing (see "Display" in `hardware/case/README.md`): the Friend's parts clear the plate by only about 0.3 mm in the model. On hardware, check the `eink-2in13-v4-portrait` overlay (which way up the text comes out: `rotation`), that the Friend's SRCS and SDCS idle high with nothing on them, and the refresh current against what REGOUT0 (+3V3 on J4) can supply alongside the other 3.3 V loads.
- **nRF52840 Dongles (PCA10059), being ordered.** Two cover the wireless tests before the boards arrive: one runs the Pup (the keyboard firmware, `boards/Pup/README.md`), the other the receiver (`dongle/README.md`).
